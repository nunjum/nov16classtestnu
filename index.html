      Overview

      A "Proposed Regulation Simulation" section where a user injects a scenario built from new intelligence about a potential regulation and sees modeled impact on supply chains and data center design.

      A "Proposed Regulation Simulation" section lets a user inject a scenario built from new intelligence about a potential regulation. The section then shows the modeled impact on supply chains and data center design. It ships with placeholder data. Students later replace that data with their own Sayari analysis of real parts.

      The section models consequences. It does not predict whether a regulation will happen.

      The demo preset is called "Next-Gen Optics Restriction" (see Scenario Simulation).

      Status: concept and specification, plus a browser-only prototype simulator (see the Proposed Regulation Simulation category). The prototype runs on placeholder data and has no server calls. The Sayari-derived values are not yet filled in. Where SOURCES.md says UNVERIFIED, the claim has not been confirmed against a primary source.

      This web page documents the concept, the specification and the sources. It is not the simulator itself.

      Nothing on this site shows that any named company is covered by a regulation or has done anything wrong. Placeholder data, where used, is labeled as placeholder and must not be read as findings about real companies.
      • Data center hardware has long lead times. Design, qualification and procurement decisions are made years before a transceiver ramps. A rule that takes effect after those decisions are locked in is expensive to absorb.
      • Regulation usually shows up first as a bill, a press report or a draft. By the time it is in force, the options for responding are narrower.
      • Supply chains are rarely visible beyond tier 1. Anticipating exposure means knowing who makes a part, who owns the manufacturer, and where else the part could be sourced.
      • Scenario work builds the habit of asking "what if" before a rule exists. It keeps that habit separate from claiming that a rule exists.

      Every item carries exactly one tier, and the tier is shown wherever the item appears. Proposed or reported items are never presented as law. Details are on the Evidence & Sources page.

      IN_FORCE

      Enacted or adopted, and currently applicable.

      PROPOSED_BILL

      Introduced legislation that has not been enacted.

      REPORTED_DRAFT

      Reported by press or sources, and not confirmed by the issuing body.

      HYPOTHETICAL

      A user-invented or exploratory assumption.

      UNVERIFIED

      A separate flag from tier: the fact has not been confirmed against a primary source.

      Component rule: the Third Report and Order prohibits authorization of devices that incorporate logic-bearing hardware components if, had the entity produced the device itself, it would be prohibited from authorization (¶2).

      • New authorizations only. ¶38: "this prohibition is effective 30 days after publication in the Federal Register, as the change only impacts new equipment authorizations." Previously authorized equipment "may continue to be marketed, imported, and used pursuant to its existing authorization." Applications pending before the effective date are exempt.
      • Hardware only. ¶30: "For now, we limit our rule to hardware components, though we keep the record open on software and firmware components."
      • Covered List entities only. ¶37: the prohibitions "apply only for logic-bearing hardware components produced by an entity identified on the Covered List." They do not apply to components from entities that only produce the production-location-based categories (UAS, UAS critical components, routers) "unless that entity is otherwise identified on the Covered List."
      • Optical transceivers. ¶22 quotes Hudson Institute commenters who list "optical transceivers" as an example of logic-bearing components. The adopted definition does not name specific component types. Whether a given transceiver meets the definition is not settled by the order text.
      • The Third FNPRM proposes further measures. Those are proposals, not part of the adopted rule (see the FNPRM entry in Sources).

      Definition of "logic-bearing hardware component" (¶28; new 47 CFR 2.902)

      any device, system, module, sub-assembly, integrated circuit, or other physical component that generates and uses timing signals or pulses at a rate in excess of 9,000 pulses (cycles) per second and uses digital techniques; inclusive of telephone equipment that uses digital techniques or any device, system, module, sub-assembly, integrated circuit, or other physical component that generates and uses radio frequency energy for the purpose of performing data processing functions

      It is based on the existing "digital device" definition in 47 CFR 15.3(k), except that it does not exclude intentional radiators. ¶28 lists non-logic-bearing examples: purely mechanical or structural parts, passive parts without digital timing signals, power-only parts, and a bare motor.

      Scenario injector inputs

      The injector is a form. Each control updates the scenario draft, and every change appends an entry to the assumption log.

      Free-text intel box

      • Plain text area for pasted intelligence (an article excerpt, a note, a bill summary).
      • It is stored verbatim as intel_text and shown beside the outputs.
      • The system does not parse it automatically in this version. The user must fill the structured fields. The UI says so.
      • Next to the box, the user must pick a tier and attach or enter a Source (type, URL or UNVERIFIED, date). The Item cannot be saved without both.

      Regulator and instrument type

      • regulator: select (FCC, Congress, DoW/DoD, Commerce, Other) plus free text.
      • instrument_type: FINAL_RULE, COVERED_LIST_ACTION, BILL, PRESS_REPORT, FNPRM, USER_HYPOTHETICAL or OTHER.
      • Guard: choosing PRESS_REPORT forces the tier to REPORTED_DRAFT or HYPOTHETICAL. Choosing BILL forces PROPOSED_BILL or HYPOTHETICAL. A FINAL_RULE may be IN_FORCE only with a primary Source.

      A single choice per Item. The choice decides which entities (and so which SKUs) the Item reaches.

      Planned outputs

      All outputs show the data-status banner (PLACEHOLDER, STUDENT_DATA or MIXED), the tier of the weakest contributing Item, and an "includes unverified input" marker when applicable.

      • Form: a grid with rows = SKUs (grouped by speed generation) and columns = 2026, 2027, 2028, 2029, 2030. Each cell shows the exposed share of that year's units. A totals row shows the portfolio total.
      • Per-cell logic: exposed units for year Y = units in Y that satisfy all of: the covered-entity test, speed-generation scope, applies-to tests, and Y at or after the effective date plus any transition. The share is exposed units divided by units in Y.
      • Spend view: a toggle switches from units to BOM spend.
      • Tier: every cell carries the weakest tier among the Items that make it non-zero. Cells driven only by a REPORTED_DRAFT Item are styled and labeled as reported. Cells driven by more than one Item list them.
      • Probability-weighted view: off by default.
      • Sources: each cell links to its contributing Items and their Sources.
      • Transition handling: exposure begins at effective_date + transition_years, if a transition applies, and exposure is zero before that. For IN_FORCE Items with no transition, exposure begins at effective_date.
      The whole panel is labeled REPORTED_DRAFT: "A US-sourced bill-of-materials exemption was reported as possible; it is unconfirmed."
      • Applies to a chosen SKU, using its BOM lines.
      • Each line has editable cost_usd, origin_country and us_sourced.
      • Calculation: US-sourced share = sum of cost_usd where us_sourced is true, divided by total cost_usd, as a percentage.
      • Comparison: the result is compared with the threshold (default 65). The exemption passes when share is greater than or equal to the threshold.
      • Display: share, threshold, margin (share minus threshold), a pass/fail marker, and a per-line contribution bar.
      • Sensitivity: "what US-sourced cost would a line need to change by to cross the threshold."
      • The definition of "US-sourced" (by value, by line) is a logged assumption, because the report's definition is not known.
      • Effect: if the toggle "apply reported exemption" is on and a SKU passes, the exposure map removes that SKU from the reported draft Item's exposure. That change is tier-capped at REPORTED_DRAFT.
      • Horizontal timeline, 2026-2030 (extendable to 2032 for the 5-year bill transition).

      Markers from Items:

      • FCC 26-50 effective 2026-10-13 IN_FORCE VERIFIED
      • S.5548 introduced 2026-09-24 PROPOSED_BILL
      • S.5548 effective at the assumed enactment date plus 5 years, shown only when the user enters an assumed enactment date HYPOTHETICAL

      Calculation rules

      Exposure for a SKU in year Y under one Item

      Exposed if all of:

      1. Item.affects_modeled_skus is true.
      2. The SKU's maker and manufacturing country satisfy the covered-entity mode.
      3. Sku.speed_generation is in the Item's scope.
      4. At least one applies-to test selects the units, using the unit counts for that test.
      5. Year Y is at or after the effective date plus transition, after date-shift.
      6. The SKU is not removed by a passing BOM exemption, when that toggle is on.

      Applies-to details

      • NEW_AUTHORIZATIONS counts units in years at or after the effective date, for SKUs where requires_fcc_authorization != NO. If the value is UNKNOWN, the cell is shown as "possibly exposed (assumption)" and the tier is capped at HYPOTHETICAL. Whether a given module or its host needs a new authorization is an input, not a finding.
      • NEW_MODELS counts all units of SKUs with is_new_model_after >= effective_date, and none of the units of older models.
      • INSTALLED_BASE counts installed_units in the first affected year.
      • FEDERAL_ONLY multiplies the units by the federal share of the SKU's zone.

      Combining Items

      Per cell, exposed share is the union of the Items that flag it (no double counting). The cell tier is the weakest tier among Items that flag the cell. A cell flagged by an IN_FORCE Item and a REPORTED_DRAFT Item shows REPORTED_DRAFT, with both listed.

      Evidence & Sources

      The evidence-tier principle, the risks of over-claiming, and the source register for the preset. Last-verified date for the register is 2026-11-16 unless an entry says otherwise.

      Every item in a scenario carries exactly one tier. The tier is shown wherever the item appears: in inputs, in outputs, and in anything exported.

      TierMeaningHow it may be presented
      IN_FORCEEnacted or adopted, and currently applicableAs current requirement, with scope limits stated
      PROPOSED_BILLIntroduced legislation that has not been enactedAs "proposed", never as law
      REPORTED_DRAFTReported by press or sources, and not confirmed by the issuing bodyAs "reported", with the outlet and a note that it is unconfirmed
      HYPOTHETICALA user-invented or exploratory assumptionAs an assumption, labeled as such

      Order from strongest to weakest: IN_FORCE > PROPOSED_BILL > REPORTED_DRAFT > HYPOTHETICAL.

      1. Never present reported or proposed items as law. No wording, color, icon or default sort order may make a PROPOSED_BILL or REPORTED_DRAFT item look like an IN_FORCE one.
      2. Tier travels with the item. Any modeled output shows the tiers of the inputs behind it. An impact driven by a REPORTED_DRAFT input is labeled as such, even when other inputs are IN_FORCE.
      3. Outputs are never stronger than inputs. Combining tiers yields the weakest tier among the contributing items, not an average.
      4. Unverified details are flagged. Where a fact is not verified, the field carries an explicit unverified marker, separate from the tier.
      5. No promotion without evidence. Moving an item to a stronger tier requires a cited primary source (a Federal Register notice, a bill text, an agency statement).

      T7verification = UNVERIFIED is a separate flag from tier. It is shown next to the tier badge, and a derived output that depends on an unverified Item inherits an "includes unverified input" marker.
      • Tier laundering. A reported item gets cited in a summary and loses its "reported" label. Mitigation: tier is part of the data, and exports carry it.
      • Scope creep. A rule limited to new authorizations and hardware is described as a general ban. Mitigation: show scope limits next to each item.
      • Naming companies. Placing a real company in a scenario can read as an accusation. Mitigation: label placeholders, cite sources for real entities, and state that being modeled is not a finding of wrongdoing or of being covered.
      • False precision. Modeled numbers look authoritative. Mitigation: show ranges or assumptions, and flag which inputs are weak-tier.
      • Treating absence as evidence. No Sayari hit does not mean no exposure. Mitigation: record coverage limits in the student write-up.
      • Stale facts. Regulatory status changes quickly. Mitigation: every item shows an "as of" date and a source, and the preset is reviewed before each use.
      • Unverified details presented as settled. The router approval expirations are one example. Mitigation: use the unverified marker, and don't remove it without a source.

      Specifications

      Goals, data model, test cases, acceptance criteria, proposed files and routes, and open questions. This is a specification only; it contains no code.

      Goals

      1. Let a user inject a scenario built from new intelligence about a potential regulation, and see the modeled impact on supply chains and data center design.
      2. Ship the "Next-Gen Optics Restriction" preset with placeholder BOM and supplier data, so the feature works before any Sayari analysis is done.

      Acceptance criteria

      1. Section exists. A "Proposed Regulation Simulation" section is reachable from the demo, with the injector and all five outputs (exposure map, BOM calculator, timeline, "what does not change" panel, assumption log).
      2. Preset loads. "Next-Gen Optics Restriction" loads with the four items (and the unverified power inverter item), each with tier, source URL (or the literal UNVERIFIED) and last-verified date, matching SOURCES.md.
      3. Tier integrity. Every Item, cell and export shows its tier. No PROPOSED_BILL or REPORTED_DRAFT content reads as law (tests EV-1 to EV-7 pass).
      4. Weakest-tier rule. Derived outputs always take the weakest contributing tier.
      5. Unverified handling. The power inverter item, the reported draft, and the router 2027-2028 expiry detail are visibly marked unverified wherever shown, and cannot be silently promoted.
      6. Injector. All inputs work, including the free-text box (stored, not auto-parsed), the three entity definitions, speed-generation scope, applies-to options, dates, transition years, the BOM threshold, and both sliders. Every change is logged.
      7. Entity definitions. Tests TH-1 to TH-8 pass, including the inclusive ownership boundary and the missing-data case.
      8. Installed base. Tests IB-1 to IB-3 pass. The default preset never counts installed base, and turning it on is logged and tier-capped.
      9. BOM calculator. Tests BOM-1 to BOM-6 pass. The threshold is editable and the panel is labeled REPORTED_DRAFT.
      10. Exposure map. It covers 2026-2030, supports a units and spend view, and shows contributing Items per cell. The conditional view is the default, and the probability-weighted view appears only on user action.
      11. Timeline. It shows the Item dates and the computed decision points, and markers move with the date-shift slider. No effective date is invented for S.5548.
      12. What does not change. The panel is always visible next to the exposure map, and its preset content matches the sourced scope limits.
      13. Design rules. D1-D6 produce suggestions with reasons and tiers. D4 never labels an architecture as exempt, and D6 capacity is always flagged as an assumption.
      14. Sayari fallback. With no Sayari tools available, the feature still runs on clearly labeled stub data, manual entry and CSV import (OT-4, OT-5). Nothing in the app depends on a specific Sayari tool name.
      15. Placeholder safety. Placeholder entities are fictional, labeled, and never presented as findings about real companies. The "not a finding of wrongdoing or coverage" statement is visible on outputs that name entities.
      16. Export. Scenario export carries tiers, verification flags, URLs, dates, settings and the assumption log (EV-7).
      17. Documentation. SOURCES.md and CONCEPT-IDEA.md are linked from the section, and the unresolved questions are listed in the repo.

      • Evidence tiers. Every item carries one tier. Items that are not IN_FORCE are always worded as "if adopted", "if enacted" or "assumed", never as current law. Results take the weakest tier of the items that produce them.
      • Statuses. Exposed, Possibly exempt (exposed unless a reported US-BOM exemption were adopted), Not affected, and Unknown (data missing). The fourth status is an addition: missing data is never treated as "not affected".
      • Matrix granularity. The matrix is by calendar year. A year counts as exposed if the exposure start date falls in or before that year.
      • Markers. † = result depends on an assumption (for example, an SKU whose FCC-authorization need is unknown). ‡ = depends on an entity flag marked UNVERIFIED.
      • Model rules follow the specification: weakest-tier combination, inclusive ownership boundary (≥ X), BOM exemption passes at ≥ threshold, sliders never change a tier.
